How much does a handyman cost in Las Vegas?

The short answer. A handyman in Las Vegas runs $50 to $90 an hour on HomeBlue’s Las Vegas page, with 4 hours at $190 to $350 plus materials. A Las Vegas operator guide puts the rate higher, at $85 to $125 an hour with a $125 to $200 minimum visit. Which guide you land on matters less than this: get the minimum and the flat price for your job in writing before the visit.

Hourly or flat — which should you pick in Las Vegas?

Flat works best when the job has a clear end. Mount this TV. Patch this hole. Swap this fan. My Las Vegas Handyman says the same on its pricing page, and its table prices those jobs flat for that reason.

Hourly works better for a list. Five small fixes, none of them big, in one visit — that is where a half day at $350 to $500, or HomeBlue’s 4-hour $190 to $350, can beat five separate minimums. This is where people overpay in Las Vegas: booking one $125 to $200 minimum visit for one $60 job, four weekends in a row.

Send photos ahead, clear the work area, and put the whole list on one visit. One minimum, once.

Desert sun writes most of the Las Vegas list

Las Vegas homes do not wear out the way homes in wet places do. The sun does the work first.

My Las Vegas Handyman’s Summerlin and Henderson repair guides name the same pattern: stucco cracks as heat and cool nights move the walls, sun-damaged fascia and trim on the sunny sides, and caulk that breaks down in a few years under UV. Gaps around windows and tubs let dust and heat in and push cooling bills up. Doors start to stick as houses settle.

That list fits the northwest valley well. In Summerlin (89128) and Centennial Hills (89129) — the two ZIPs the featured business below covers — a typical visit is stucco touch-ups, fresh caulk, a sticking door, and a fan swap. Bundle them. Each one alone hits the minimum. Together they fill a half day at a better rate.

Check the south and west sides first. That is where the sun hits hardest, and where a small crack becomes a bigger patch after monsoon rain gets in.

The Nevada $1,000 rule — know it before you book

Nevada has no separate handyman license. Under the Nevada State Contractors Board exemption in NRS 624.031, a handyman can take repair and maintenance work under $1,000 total, labor and materials combined, as long as the job needs no building permit and is not part of a bigger project.

Cross that line and you need a licensed contractor. Plumbing, electrical, HVAC and refrigeration work are also outside the exemption — those need a licensed trade contractor at any price, per Housecall Pro’s Nevada licensing guide.

So ask two plain questions before you book a bigger list: is the whole project, parts included, under $1,000, and does any part of it need a permit or a licensed trade? If the answer to either is yes, you are not hiring a handyman for that job. You are hiring a contractor.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a handyman cost in Las Vegas?

HomeBlue puts a Las Vegas handyman at $50 to $90 an hour, with 4 hours at $190 to $350 plus materials. My Las Vegas Handyman puts Las Vegas rates at $85 to $125 an hour with a $125 to $200 minimum visit. Handy702, a Las Vegas operator, charges $100 an hour with a 1-hour minimum.

How much does it cost to mount a TV in Las Vegas?

My Las Vegas Handyman prices TV mounting on a standard wall at $150 to $300. Block, stone or tile walls, high mounting, and hiding the cords take longer and cost more, per that same pricing page.

Is a half day of handyman work worth it in Las Vegas?

Usually, if you have a list. My Las Vegas Handyman prices a half day (4 hours) of small repairs at $350 to $500, and HomeBlue prices 4 hours in Las Vegas at $190 to $350 plus materials. Either way, one half day beats several separate visits, each with its own minimum.

What jobs come up most in Las Vegas homes?

Stucco cracks, sun-damaged fascia and trim, failed caulk around windows and tubs, drywall cracks, and sticking doors, per My Las Vegas Handyman’s Summerlin and Henderson repair guides. Desert sun, heat and dry air drive most of that list.

When does a Las Vegas job need a licensed contractor instead of a handyman?

When the whole project is $1,000 or more in labor and materials combined, when it needs a building permit, or when it is plumbing, electrical, HVAC or refrigeration work. Nevada’s handyman exemption under NRS 624.031 covers only smaller, no-permit repair and maintenance work.

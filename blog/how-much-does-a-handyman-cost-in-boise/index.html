How much does a handyman cost in Boise?

The short answer. In Boise, a handyman runs $40–$80 an hour, plus materials. A half-day list runs $170–$330, and a full day runs $350–$650 (HomeBlue, Boise). One fall job has its own flat price here: a sprinkler blowout runs $65–$85 for 1–5 zones, and it costs more the later you book it.

This is where people overpay. A one-hour job rarely bills at one hour. Nationally, most handymen set a minimum of $75–$200 and a trip charge of $30–$80 (HomeGuide, national). A 20-minute fix booked alone pays that floor. On a half-day list, it costs the hourly rate and nothing extra.

What changes the price in Boise?

The list, or the lack of one. One loose cabinet hinge in Meridian (83646) is a minimum-charge visit. That hinge plus a drywall patch, a running toilet, and a ceiling fan swap is one trip and one setup at $170–$330 for the half day. Write the small jobs down as they happen and hand over the whole list at once.

Fall comes fast in the Treasure Valley. The big job here in late October is the sprinkler blowout — clearing water from irrigation lines before the first hard freeze. The window runs October through mid-November, and a hard freeze below 28°F often lands in late October (Lawn Master, Boise). Booking early pays: The Maintenance Shop in Meridian charges $65 for 1–5 zones early, $75 Nov 10–30, and $85 from Dec 1. Skip it and freeze damage runs $500–$1,500 or more (Lawn Master).

Idaho draws the line at $2,000. A handyman in Idaho does not need state registration for a job under $2,000 in labor and materials combined. At $2,000 or more, he has to register with the Idaho Contractors Board before he starts. Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work need their own trade license no matter what the job costs. So a handyman can patch, hang, assemble, and repair all day — rewiring a panel or moving a gas line is a licensed trade's job, not a cheap add-on.

Who you hire. Nationally, a self-employed handyman runs $50–$80 an hour and a corporate handyman runs $75–$125 (HomeGuide, national). Boise's $40–$80 range sits at the solo end. A company crew costs more and usually guarantees the work.

How to pay less without a worse job

  • Bundle the list. One four-hour visit at $170–$330 costs less than four separate calls, each hitting a minimum.
  • Book fall work before the price steps up. A blowout booked early is $65 in Meridian. The same job in December is $85 — and a frozen line costs hundreds.
  • Buy the parts yourself when you can. A handyman who buys materials may add a 20% to 50% markup, plus a trip charge (HomeGuide, national). The fan, faucet, or hinge sitting by the door removes both.
  • Ask the price shape before he starts. Hourly, flat per job, or hourly plus a minimum — all three are normal in Boise. Not knowing which one you agreed to is where most bad bills come from.

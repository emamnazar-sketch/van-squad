How much does mobile pet grooming cost in Kansas City, Kansas?

The short answer. It depends on which kind of mobile you book. A Kansas City van company, Pet Rinse Repeat, bills haircuts and desheds at $130 to $170 an hour, plus a $20 travel fee, one-hour minimum, most dogs done in one to two hours. An in-home groomer in Lenexa charges a flat $75 to $140 for a full groom by weight. HomeGuide’s Overland Park page puts a mobile full service at about $75 for an average-size dog. Ask which model your quote uses first.

This is where people overpay. A $75 quote and a $200 quote can both be honest here. One is a flat price in your own tub in Lenexa. The other is an hour and a half in a van at $130 an hour, plus $20 travel. Know the model — hourly or flat, travel in or out — before you compare.

Two pricing models, and why the gap is real

Hourly van work. Pet Rinse Repeat serves Kansas City, KS from a van: $130 to $170 an hour, time past the first hour billed in 15-minute steps, $20 travel fee on top. Two hours of coat pays for two hours.

Flat by weight, in your home. Furever Family Dog Care in Lenexa grooms in your home — it needs a tub and two outlets — and charges flat by size: $75 up to 15 pounds, $140 at 51 to 70 pounds. No travel fee is listed.

Individual groomers sit below both. Care.com lists individual groomers in Overland Park at an average posted rate of $16.00 an hour, and in Kansas City, KS at $9.00 an hour — light grooming alongside sitting and walking, not a van company.

What changes the price in Johnson County?

Size first. On the Lenexa table, a full groom nearly doubles from the smallest dog to a 51-to-70-pound dog, $75 to $140. On the van, size shows up as time — bigger dog, longer hour count.

Coat and matting. A doodle or double coat takes longer than a flat coat. That’s why Pet Rinse Repeat prices oodles at $140 an hour and large desheds at $170, above its $130 rate. Its flat-coat bath is its one flat rate, from $100.

Ticks run most of the year here. College Boulevard Animal Hospital in Overland Park, citing Kansas State University Extension in Johnson County, puts tick activity in the Kansas City area from March through November, peaking in spring and fall, and recommends year-round prevention. The American dog tick is the most common in Kansas on its list. HomeGuide prices a flea & tick treatment at $15 — for a dog who walks wooded trails and yard edges, that’s a regular line item.

The wait. Pet Rinse Repeat says mobile appointments typically book one to three weeks out, with a three-hour arrival window. Most clients keep a recurring four-, six-, or eight-week schedule.

Mobile or salon — what does the extra money buy?

A salon full groom in Overland Park costs $65 at Pawz at Play, in ZIP 66210, wash and dry $30. HomeGuide starts a salon full service at $40 for a small dog, $75 for a large one — less than a van hour.

What the van money buys is the trip you don’t make and the wait your dog doesn’t do. No drive, no cage, one dog start to finish at your curb. For a dog who hates the car, that’s the point. For a calm dog and a tight budget, the salon still wins.

How to pay less and still get a good groom

  • Ask the model before the price. Hourly or flat? Travel fee in or out? Those answers explain most of the gap between quotes.
  • Stay on a regular schedule. Care.com’s Overland Park page notes long-haired and double-coated dogs do best every four to six weeks. A kept-up coat grooms faster — cheaper on an hourly van.
  • Brush at home between visits. Small tangles are free to fix on your porch. Matting in the van is billed time.
  • Price the add-ons up front. On these Kansas lists the small pieces run $10 to $22 each. They only sting when each one is a surprise.

# How much does a handyman cost in Oklahoma City?

## The short answer

In Oklahoma City, a handyman costs about **$40 to $80 an hour** (HomeBlue). A half day — 4 hours — runs **$170 to $310**, plus materials, and a full 8-hour day runs **$330 to $620**. Small, predictable jobs are often flat-priced instead: on Thumbtack's Edmond page, examples include **$75 for a toilet replacement or a ceiling fan and light fixture, and $125 for a faucet replacement**, with minimum rates for handyman jobs typically **$60–$100**.

## What changes the price in Oklahoma City?

**The list, not just the hour.** One visit that covers a running toilet, a sticking door, and a drywall patch in the same home spreads one minimum fee across three jobs. Three separate visits pay that minimum three times — Thumbtack itself gives bundling as its cost-saving tip for handyman work.

**Where you live in the metro.** Van Squads lists handymen covering Oklahoma City itself (73114) and Moore (73160), just south of the city. Homes inside a handyman's regular route rarely see a travel charge; jobs well outside it — out toward Edmond or Norman — are more likely to carry a higher minimum.

**Skill and licensing.** Basic repairs and installs sit at the low end of the range. A local OKC rates page prices carpentry higher, at $70–$130 an hour, and simple electrical or plumbing tasks at $75–$150 an hour — and bigger electrical or plumbing work legally needs a licensed trade, not a handyman.

**Storm-season timing.** Oklahoma City's spring storm season leaves a trail of loose fence gates, damaged trim, and small exterior repairs across the metro, and handymen book up fast in the weeks after a storm. A non-urgent list waits cheaper in a quiet week than in the first week after high winds.

## How to pay less without getting worse work

- **Bundle the list.** Write down every small repair first, then book one visit for all of it — this is the single biggest saving on handyman work.
- **Supply your own materials.** Buy the faucet, fan, or fixture yourself when you can, and avoid the 10–30% materials markup.
- **Get the price shape in writing.** Ask whether you are paying hourly, flat per job, or hourly plus a minimum — and what that minimum is — before the visit starts.

## What changes the price in Sioux Falls?

**The list, or the lack of one.** Write the small jobs down as they happen. In ZIPs 57103 through 57110, one visit can cover a running toilet, a loose railing, and a ceiling fan. Booked one at a time, each one pays its own minimum.

**November is the last easy month before winter sets in.** Gutter cleaning in Sioux Falls runs $70–$170 for a typical one- or two-story home (HomeBlue, Sioux Falls). Full gutters freeze that way, and ice is harder on a roof edge than leaves are. The same visit is the time to get exposed pipes wrapped and drafty doors sealed — small jobs now, not a burst pipe in January.

**South Dakota doesn't license handymen at the state level.** No state handyman license, and no state general contractor license either. Sioux Falls is stricter on bigger work: contractors taking on jobs that need a permit have to hold the city's Residential Building Contractor License, and repair work needs a state Contractor's Excise Tax License. Electrical and plumbing carry their own trade licenses. A handyman can patch, hang, assemble, and repair — new wiring or a gas line is a licensed trade's job.

**Who you hire.** Nationally, a self-employed handyman runs $50–$80 an hour and a company handyman runs $75–$125 (HomeGuide, national). Sioux Falls' $40–$80 range covers the solo end and part of the company end. A company costs more and usually guarantees the work. A solo operator costs less and books out faster in fall.

## How to pay less without a worse job

- **Bundle the list.** One four-hour visit at $170–$320 beats four separate calls, each hitting a $60–$100 minimum.
- **Get the fall work done before snow.** Gutters at $70–$170 are November prices in Sioux Falls. Ice and a ladder in January are a different quote.
- **Buy the parts yourself when you can.** A handyman who buys materials may add a 20% to 50% markup, plus a trip charge (HomeGuide, national). The faucet or disposal sitting by the door removes both.
- **Ask the price shape before he starts.** Hourly, flat per job, or hourly plus a minimum — all three are normal in Sioux Falls. Not knowing which one you agreed to is where most bad bills come from.

# How much should I tip a mobile car detailer?

## The short answer

Tip **15–20% of the service price** for good work — that is $30–$40 on a $200 detail. Tipping is not required, but it is the standard way to say the job was done well: $10–$20 is fair on a small $99 wash, and $50–$70 on a $349 full detail. If the work was only satisfactory, 10–15% is fine; if it was genuinely bad, skip the tip and tell the business why.

## Do you have to tip a mobile detailer?

No — tipping is not required, but it is always appreciated. A mobile detailer drives to you, brings their own water, power, and equipment, and usually works alone for 2–5 hours on your car by hand. The price pays for the job; the tip marks that this person did it well.

## How much is the right tip for each job?

Use the percentage, then check it against these real 2026 job prices:

| What you booked | Typical price | Standard tip (15–20%) |
|---|---|---|
| Basic wash & wax | $99 | $10–$20 |
| Detail around $200 | $200 | $30–$40 |
| Full interior detail | $249 | $35–$50 |
| Full interior + exterior detail | $349 | $50–$70 |

Tip on the full price of the service, and land at the top of the range when the detailer went beyond the package — a stubborn stain removed, pet hair fully cleared, or a car that came back looking better than you expected.

## What should push your tip up?

- **A messy vehicle.** Heavy pet hair, ground-in dirt, stains, or a car that has not been cleaned in years means real extra labor — tip toward 20% or above.
- **Long, skilled work.** Paint correction and ceramic coating are precision jobs — a coating alone can take 8–12 hours. Tips there are especially appreciated.
- **Hard conditions.** Full sun, bad weather, or a tight parking spot with no easy water or power access — and they still delivered.
- **They came to you.** Travel time and setup are part of the mobile premium — the at-home visit is a reason to tip slightly more, not less.

## When is it okay not to tip?

Skip the tip when the service itself failed: areas were missed, the result did not match what was promised, something was damaged, or the detailer was late with no communication or unprofessional. A tip signals approval — if you do not have it, do not send it. Give the business a chance to make the job right instead. The one other exception: a corporate account on a fixed, all-inclusive rate, where gratuity may already be built in — check before adding more.

## Do you tip a detailer who owns the business?

It is personal preference, and either choice is reasonable. Some customers skip it because owners set their own prices; others tip because the labor and quality are the same whoever owns the van. If you do not tip an owner-operator, an honest review helps just as much — reviews and referrals bring small mobile businesses their next customer.

## How do you actually pay the tip?

Cash is still the cleanest option — it goes straight to the person who did the work, with no fees or delays. Have $15–$30 ready for a basic service and $40–$60 for a full detail. No cash? Most independent detailers take Venmo, Zelle, or Cash App — ask which they prefer — and card payments usually show a tip prompt at checkout. Forgot entirely? Sending it digitally within a day or two is fine.

## FAQ

**How much should I tip a mobile car detailer?**
15–20% of the service price for good work — $30–$40 on a $200 detail. Tip 10–15% if the work was just satisfactory, and more than 20% when the detailer handled a very messy car or did exceptional work.

**Is tipping a mobile detailer required?**
No. It is customary and appreciated, never an obligation. If the work was incomplete or something was damaged, skip the tip and contact the business so they can make it right.

**How much do you tip on a $99 basic wash?**
$10–$20. Small jobs are where a percentage gets tiny, so a flat amount in that range is the norm — detailers suggest keeping $15–$30 in cash ready for a basic service.

**Should you tip more because the detailer came to your house?**
Yes — tip toward the top of the range. The detailer added travel time, loaded and unloaded their own equipment, and worked around your parking situation to save you the trip.

**Do you tip a mobile detailer who owns the business?**
Only if you want to — either choice is reasonable. Owners set their own prices, so a tip is never expected, but the work is the same. An honest review is an equally valued alternative.

# How much does a handyman cost in Albuquerque?

## The short answer

In Albuquerque, a handyman runs **$40–$80 an hour**, plus materials. A half-day list — four hours of small fixes — runs **$160–$300**, and a full eight-hour day runs **$320–$610**. Most people overpay on the small end, not the big end: a 20-minute fix billed alone still carries the trip and the minimum, so one visit with a list beats three visits with one job each.

## What changes the price in Albuquerque?

**The list, or the lack of one.** One sticking door in 87112 is a minimum-charge visit. That same door plus a drywall patch, a loose gate latch, and two curtain rods is one trip and one setup. Albuquerque homes collect the same small jobs — stucco cracks, a cooler pad change in spring, a door that swells and sticks after the monsoon rains. Write them down as they happen and hand over the whole list at once.

**Adobe, stucco, and flat roofs.** A lot of Albuquerque houses are stucco over block or frame, and patching stucco so it blends is slower than patching plain drywall. Flat-roof homes also turn a small ceiling stain into a look-up-top-first job. None of that is exotic. It just means a Northeast Heights stucco patch can take longer than the national drywall number suggests.

**Licensed work is not handyman work in New Mexico.** This one matters here. New Mexico's handyman exemption excludes electrical, plumbing, and gas fitting — that work needs a licensed trade, whatever the size of the job. A handyman can hang the ceiling fan you bought. Rewiring the box it hangs from is a licensed electrician's job. If a quote folds real electrical or plumbing work into a cheap hourly rate, that is a reason to pass, not a bargain.

**Distance across town.** Albuquerque is spread out. A handyman based in the Northeast who drives to the Westside or down to the South Valley burns unpaid time in the truck, and some charge for it. The two ZIPs our featured listing covers — 87112 in the northeast and 87113 on the far north side — are close together, which is part of why local pros price by the area they actually work.

## How to pay less without a worse job

- **Bundle the list.** One four-hour visit at $160–$300 costs less than four separate one-hour calls, each hitting a minimum.
- **Buy the parts yourself when you can.** Nationally, a handyman who buys materials may add a 20% to 50% markup, plus a trip charge (HomeGuide, national). The faucet, fan, or latch sitting by the door when he arrives removes both.
- **Ask the price shape before he starts.** Hourly, flat per job, or hourly plus a minimum — all three are normal. Not knowing which one you agreed to is where most bad bills come from.

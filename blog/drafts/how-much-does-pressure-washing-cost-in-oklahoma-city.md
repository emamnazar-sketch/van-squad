## What changes the price in Oklahoma City?

**Red dirt and storm mud.** Spring storms throw Oklahoma red dirt onto driveways, sidewalks, and the bottom of your siding. It stains. A driveway that just needs a rinse is a short job. One with red dirt worked into the concrete, plus oil spots, takes a pre-treatment and a second pass.

**Heat, shade, and green growth.** North-facing walls and shaded patios in older Oklahoma City neighborhoods stay damp longer, and that is where algae shows up green and black. That work is soft washing, not high pressure — priced for the solution and dwell time, not just the water.

**The surface itself.** Concrete can take pressure. Vinyl, stucco, wood, and painted siding cannot. Elite's Oklahoma City list shows the spread: sidewalks at $0.15–$0.30 a square foot, building exteriors at $0.20–$0.50, roof and awning cleaning at $0.30–$0.60. Height adds to it — a two-story wash in Edmond or Norman costs more than a one-story.

## How to pay less without tearing up your siding

- **Bundle the flat work in one visit.** Driveway, sidewalk, and patio in one stop beats three separate trips, each with its own minimum.
- **Book after storm season, not in the middle of it.** Thumbtack's Oklahoma City guidance is a wash at least once a year, best after the rainy storm season has passed, on a warm day that lets the house dry. Wash too early and the next storm undoes it.
- **Do the small concrete yourself, hire out the house.** Angi puts a pro wash at around $300 nationally, a rental at about $90 a day, and siding repair at nearly $900 if you puncture it yourself. Renting for a driveway makes sense. A ladder and a two-story wall is a bad trade.
- **Ask soft wash or pressure, in writing.** If a quote does not say which method goes on your siding and roof, you do not actually have the same job priced twice.

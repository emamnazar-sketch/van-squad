# How much does a handyman cost in Portland?

## The short answer

In Portland, a handyman runs **$50–$90 an hour**, plus materials (HomeBlue, Portland). A half day — four hours — runs **$200–$370**, and a full day runs **$390–$740**. Angi prices the typical project a different way: **$382 on average, most jobs between $156 and $611**. Established firms sit above that range — one Portland operator charges **$125 an hour with a two-hour minimum**.

## What changes the price in Portland?

**The wet season writes half the list.** Angi's Portland page puts exterior weatherproofing right up front — Portland handymen do a lot of it ahead of the wet, cool winters. It also flags a detail most guides skip: weather stripping work can need extra prep to dry out damp frames after heavy rain. A door that sticks in November in Powellhurst-Gilbert (97236) is often a swollen, damp frame, not a bad door. Drying and sealing take time a dry July frame doesn't.

**Who you hire moves the rate more than the job does.** HomeGuide puts a self-employed handyman at $50–$80 an hour nationally and a company handyman at $75–$125; Portland homeowners report $55–$80 in HomeGuide's Portland row. Neil Kelly, a Portland firm, charges $125 an hour with a two-hour minimum — a short job there starts at $250 before materials. A solo operator may do the same fix inside the $50–$90 HomeBlue range. The difference buys the office, the insurance, and a warranty behind the work.

**Minimums decide what one small job costs.** Angi notes Portland pros charge a minimum call-out fee covering the first hour, so even a 10-minute job pays that base charge. Nationally, minimums run $75–$200, with a $30–$80 trip charge for some pros (HomeGuide, national). One fix pays the whole minimum. Four fixes on one visit split it.

**Materials markup isn't one number.** If your handyman buys the parts, Angi puts the Portland markup at 10% to 30% above retail. HomeGuide's national figure is higher — 20% to 50%, plus that trip charge. Ask which one your quote uses. A faucet or deadbolt sitting by the door when he arrives removes the question.

**Oregon licenses this work more strictly than most states.** Oregon doesn't hand out a separate handyman license. Paid construction work generally needs a Construction Contractors Board (CCB) license, and the only exemption is narrow: casual, minor work under $1,000 total, labor and materials combined. Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work need their own trade licenses no matter the price. HomeBlue's Portland guide adds that many small jobs need no permit, but an exterior door replacement sometimes does, and bigger decks can too.

## How to pay less without a worse job

- **Bundle the list.** One four-hour visit at $200–$370 beats four separate calls, each paying its own first-hour minimum.
- **Do the weather work before the rain settles in.** A damp door frame costs more prep time than a dry one.
- **Buy the parts yourself when you can.** That skips the 10% to 30% Portland markup Angi reports, and the shopping trip behind it.
- **Ask the price shape before he starts.** Hourly, flat per job, or hourly plus a minimum — all three are normal in Portland. Ask for the CCB number on any job that grows past small repairs.

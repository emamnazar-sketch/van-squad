# How much does a handyman cost per hour?

## The short answer

Most handymen charge **$50–$150 per hour** in 2026, with **$75–$90 per hour** the typical average rate. Almost every visit carries a minimum — usually **$125–$250** for the first 1–2 hours — and the average handyman project totals **$405**, with most homeowners paying **$164–$647**. One short fix costs the minimum; a bundled list of small jobs is where the hourly rate pays off.

## What does the hourly rate depend on — who you hire?

The biggest split is independent versus company. The work is the same; the overhead is not.

| Who you hire | Typical 2026 hourly rate |
|---|---|
| Self-employed handyman | $50–$80 |
| Handyman company / franchise | $75–$125 |
| Any handyman, full range | $50–$150 |

Franchise and company crews run higher because insurance, guarantees, and office costs are built in. Experienced, licensed, or insured independents charge toward the top of their range too — especially for specialized or riskier work.

## What do common jobs cost as a flat price?

For jobs with a clear scope, many handymen quote a flat price instead of an hourly rate:

| Job | Typical 2026 flat price |
|---|---|
| Light fixture replacement | $65–$175 |
| Door hardware repair | $75–$220 |
| Furniture assembly | $85–$200 |
| Ceiling fan installation | $100–$400 |
| Faucet replacement | $120–$300 |
| TV mounting | $150–$400 |
| Drywall repair | $200–$800 |

By visit size, a simple task under 2 hours typically totals $178–$220, a few tasks over 2–5 hours run $316–$428, and a full day or complex project runs $701–$1,105.

## Why is there a minimum charge for a 20-minute job?

Because the trip is the same however short the fix is. Minimum service fees run **$125–$200** to cover travel, setup, and admin time, and some pros add a **$30–$80 trip fee** outside their normal area. That is why one tiny job always looks expensive per minute — and why bundling several small jobs into one visit is the standard way to beat the minimum: you pay the trip cost once, not five times.

## What pushes the price up or down?

- **Location.** Large coastal cities run $90–$150 per hour; rural areas run $45–$70. High-cost-of-living areas simply cost more.
- **Job type.** Electrical, plumbing, or carpentry work costs more than basic repairs — it takes more skill and carries more risk.
- **Materials.** If the handyman buys materials for you, expect a **10–30% markup** over retail. Supplying your own is one of the easiest ways to pay less.
- **Timing and access.** Emergency, weekend, and after-hours work costs more, as does work in attics, crawl spaces, or other hard-to-reach spots. Prep, cleanup, and debris disposal can be billed on top.

## What can a handyman not legally do?

A handyman covers small repairs and installs: TV mounting, furniture assembly, drywall patches, painting, caulking, door adjustments, and replacing existing light fixtures or faucets. In most states, work beyond that belongs to a licensed trade: running new wiring, rerouting plumbing, HVAC installation, gas work, structural changes, and anything that needs a permit. A handyman who offers to do permit work without a license is a red flag, not a bargain.

## How do you keep the cost down?

Bundle your small jobs into one visit, supply your own materials where you can, and clear the work area before the handyman arrives so prep is not billed as labor. Get more than one quote, state the scope clearly up front to avoid change orders, and ask about a flat rate for a half-day or full-day list — it usually beats paying the minimum again and again.

## FAQ

**How much does a handyman cost per hour?**
$50–$150 per hour in 2026. Self-employed handymen typically charge $50–$80, companies and franchises $75–$125, and $75–$90 is the typical average rate nationwide.

**How much does a small handyman job cost in total?**
Expect the minimum: most pros charge a $125–$200 minimum service fee, and a simple task under 2 hours typically totals $178–$220 once the visit is billed.

**Is it cheaper to pay hourly or a flat rate?**
Flat rate suits a single, well-defined job — a TV mount or a faucet swap — because you know the total before work starts. Hourly suits a mixed list of small jobs or a problem nobody can scope until it is opened up.

**Do you tip a handyman?**
It is not mandatory. For small jobs a tip of $10–$20 is common, and $20–$50 for larger or more complex projects. A good review or a referral to a neighbor is also widely appreciated.

**How much does a handyman charge for a full day?**
Day rates run $450–$1,000 for 8 hours, with most flat day rates at $500–$800. Filling the day with a list of 6–10 small tasks makes a full-day visit far cheaper per task than separate calls.

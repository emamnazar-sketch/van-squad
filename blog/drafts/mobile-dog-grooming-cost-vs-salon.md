# How Much Does Mobile Dog Grooming Cost Compared to a Salon?

## The short answer

Mobile grooming runs about **20–30% more** than an equivalent salon visit. In 2026, expect roughly **$60–$90** for a small dog, **$80–$130** for a medium dog, and **$100–$160+** for a large dog for a full mobile groom. The premium covers the groomer's van, fuel, travel time, and one-on-one attention — no kennels, no other dogs, no driving across town.

## What does mobile dog grooming cost in 2026?

Here's what full-groom pricing looks like right now, side by side:

| Dog size | Salon full groom | Mobile full groom |
|---|---|---|
| Small (under 20 lbs) | $40–$80 | $60–$90 |
| Medium (20–50 lbs) | $65–$110 | $80–$130 |
| Large (50–80 lbs) | $90–$140 | $100–$160 |
| XL / giant (80+ lbs) | $120–$180+ | $150–$220+ |

A bath-only visit (no haircut) runs cheaper: about $25–$75 at a salon depending on size, versus $65–$110+ from a mobile groomer for small and medium dogs. Heavy-coated breeds like doodles sit at the top end — a full mobile groom for a doodle or XL breed can reach **$190–$260+**.

Two things move any quote up fast: a **matted coat** (typically a $15–$50 surcharge, since de-matting adds real time) and an **anxious or difficult dog** (more time, more care). When you ask for a quote, describe your dog's actual coat condition — not just the breed. A groomer quoting $60 for a "small dog" and meeting a badly matted Maltese will add charges, and fairly so.

## Why does mobile grooming cost more than a salon?

It's not markup for the sake of it. A mobile groomer runs a fully equipped van — water tanks, dryers, clippers, climate control — and drives it to your driveway. That means fuel, vehicle maintenance, insurance on the van, and unpaid travel time between appointments. A salon spreads those costs across many dogs per day; a mobile groomer does fewer appointments, one dog at a time.

What you're buying with the premium: your dog never sits in a kennel between stages, never deals with a loud salon full of strange dogs, and gets the groomer's undivided attention for the whole appointment. For many owners, that's the entire point.

## Is mobile grooming worth the extra cost?

It depends on your dog — and your schedule. Mobile grooming earns its premium fastest in three situations:

- **Anxious or reactive dogs.** No car ride, no waiting in a cage, no other animals. For dogs that shake at the salon door, the calmer setting alone is worth it.
- **Senior or special-needs dogs.** Less handling, less waiting, no slippery salon floors for arthritic legs.
- **Your own time.** No drop-off, no pickup window, no sitting in the parking lot. The groomer texts you when they're done and your dog walks back inside.

One honest trade-off: availability. Mobile groomers in most metro markets are **booking weeks to months ahead**, and some have closed their books to new clients entirely. If you find a good one with an opening, lock in a recurring slot.

## How can I pay less for mobile grooming?

- **Book on a regular schedule.** Dogs groomed every 4–6 weeks take less time per visit — and many groomers discount standing appointments.
- **Brush at home between visits.** Daily brushing on a doodle or long-coated breed is the single cheapest way to avoid the $15–$50 matting surcharge.
- **Ask about bundles.** Bath, nail trim, and brushing booked together often costs less than adding services one by one.
- **Compare local quotes.** Prices vary a lot by market — in major metros, add 20–40% to national averages; in smaller towns, expect less.

## What should I ask before booking a mobile groomer?

- What's included in the base price — and what counts as an add-on?
- How do you handle matted coats or anxious dogs, and what are the extra charges?
- Is the van fully self-contained, or do you need anything from my home?
- Can I see recent reviews or before-and-after photos?
- What's your cancellation policy?

## FAQ

**How much should I tip a mobile dog groomer?**
15–20% of the service price, the same standard as salons and hair stylists. Cash is preferred — it goes straight to the groomer.

**Why is grooming a doodle so expensive?**
Curly doodle coats mat fast and take 60–90 minutes of clipper work alone. A doodle groom runs $80–$150+ and takes 2–3 hours, which is why they're the priciest common breed to maintain.

**How often should my dog be groomed?**
Every 4–6 weeks for long or curly coats (doodles, Shih Tzus), every 8–10 weeks for heavy shedders like Huskies, and every 8–12 weeks for short-coated breeds.

**Are mobile groomers really booked out for months?**
In many metro areas, yes. Mobile is the fastest-growing grooming segment and demand has outrun supply — book early and keep a recurring slot once you have one.

**Does a matted coat cost extra?**
Almost always. Expect a $15–$50 de-matting surcharge; severe matting may require a full shave-down at additional cost.

# How much does pressure washing cost in Denver?

## The short answer

In Denver, pressure washing a house averages **$295** on Angi, with most homes at **$170 to $420**. HomeBlue's Denver guide puts the same job higher — about **$600 on average, $400 to $700 for a 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft house**. A driveway averages about **$230** on HomeBlue, **$160 to $290 for a standard 40 x 20 ft driveway**. Angi puts Denver about 5% under the national average. Add tax — Denver's rate is 9.15%, about $25 on a typical job.

## What changes the price in Denver?

**Winter road chemicals, mostly.** A Denver metro operator puts it plainly: magnesium chloride and calcium chloride go on metro roads from October through April, get tracked onto driveways, and corrode concrete if they sit there. That's why the spring wash in Lakewood (80226) isn't just about looks. It's getting a corrosive film off before another freeze-thaw season works it into cracks.

**The Front Range adds its own grime.** The same operator lists red clay soil that bonds hard to concrete, hard to very hard Denver water that leaves a white mineral haze, and high-altitude sun that roughens surfaces so they trap more dirt. A driveway with white haze and clay staining is pre-treatment and dwell time, not a quick rinse — and it prices that way.

**Stories and size set the base.** HomeBlue climbs by house size, up to $1,000 to $1,800 at 4,000 to 5,000 sq ft. Angi prices two-story Denver homes at $400 to $1,400 against $150 to $1,000 for one story. Height, tight access, and limited water access push a quote up.

**The surface decides the method, and the method sets the price.** Angi prices Denver work by material per square foot: brick $0.10 to $0.40, vinyl $0.20 to $0.50, stucco $0.30 to $0.50, wood $0.30 to $0.55. Concrete takes high pressure. Siding gets a soft wash — lower pressure, with the cleaning mix doing the work — because a hard blast can damage it.

## Should you pay for hot water?

Sometimes. HomeBlue notes power washing — heated water — costs 10 to 15% more than its cold-water Denver prices. The Denver metro operator uses hot water at 180 to 200°F and says it earns that on oil and grease, where cold water alone can't lift the stain, and on road-chemical residue.

On plain dust and winter film, it usually doesn't earn it. Angi's Denver pros charge $50 to $150 an hour, and renting a machine yourself runs $50 to $250 a day — before you count your own Saturday. If the quote adds a hot-water charge, ask which stain it is for.

## How to pay less and still get it clean

- **Bundle surfaces in one visit.** House, driveway, and front walk in one stop — the crew is already set up. A 200 sq ft deck or patio runs $70 to $110 as its own job on HomeBlue; as an add-on it should cost less.
- **Wash in spring, after the road-chemical season.** Angi puts the best window at March to November, with driveways in spring or fall — spring in Denver means after the last mag chloride, not before it.
- **Get the measuring stick in writing.** By house size, by story, or per square foot — and per square foot of what? The two Denver guides differ mostly on this, not on the work.
- **Send photos of the worst spots.** The white mineral haze, the clay-stained edge, the oil spot by the garage in Lakewood (80226). A photo quote beats a surprise on the day.

**How much does pressure washing cost in Denver?**

About $230 on average for a 40 x 20 ft driveway, $160 to $290 (HomeBlue). Angi puts Denver driveways at $100 to $250, and a Denver metro operator at $150 to $350 by size and staining.

**How often should you pressure wash in Denver, and when?**
Once a year (Angi), in the March to November window — siding and driveways in spring or fall. Window Genie of Denver advises every 12 to 18 months. A Denver metro operator recommends an annual driveway wash, with the spring clean doing the important work: removing winter road chemicals before they damage the concrete.

**Should my siding be pressure washed or soft washed in Denver?**
Soft washed. A Denver metro operator matches the method to the surface — siding and wood get lower pressure with a pre-treatment mix, while concrete and masonry take 2,500 to 3,500 PSI.

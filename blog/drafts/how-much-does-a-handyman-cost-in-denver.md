# How much does a handyman cost in Denver?

In Denver, a handyman runs **$50 to $90 an hour** on HomeBlue, with **4 hours at $190 to $350** and **8 hours at $370 to $700**, plus materials. One small job alone almost always pays a minimum. A list bundled into one visit is where your money stretches.

## What changes the price in Denver?

**The type of shop sets the hourly band.** EvenQuote splits Denver cleanly: an independent runs $60 to $88 an hour, a local company $88 to $120, and a franchise $100 to $155. HomeBlue's $50 to $90 range covers the independent end of that same market. You're not always buying better work at the top band. Sometimes you're buying a call center and a warranty desk.

**Front Range weather writes a lot of the list.** One metro operator with 15+ years here puts it this way: Front Range winters, hail seasons, and bone-dry summers are what a Colorado house has to hold together against. In Aurora (80013), that shows up as cracked caulk, loose fence pickets, and doors that fit in May and stick by January. All one-hour jobs — if they're on the same list.

**Materials sit outside the hourly rate.** HomeBlue's Denver table is labor only. Both metro operators above bill materials at cost, with no markup and no travel fee in Aurora. Ask before anyone starts: what's the hourly rate, and how are materials billed?

**Licensed work and permits push a job out of handyman pricing.** HomeBlue notes your price rises if the task needs a trade license, special equipment, or a permit. That changes who can legally do the work, and what the visit costs.

## What can a handyman legally do in Denver?

Colorado doesn't require a state handyman license for general repair and maintenance work. Denver Handy Co. spells out the other half: licensing is required for electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work, and for projects that need permits.

HomeBlue gives two permit examples worth knowing: an exterior door replacement sometimes needs a permit in Denver, and decks of a certain height and size can need one too. A faucet swap, a patch, a curtain rod — that's handyman work. New wiring or a moved drain isn't, no matter how small it looks on your list.

## How to pay less and still get it done right

- **Hand over the whole list at once.** Handy Andy says most customers stack several small jobs into one visit, and with hourly billing that's the cheapest way to clear a list. His typical times help you build one — a leaky faucet is 1 to 2 hours, flat-pack assembly 1 to 3, a patch with paint touch-up 1 to 4.
- **Use the minimum, don't fight it.** If the visit minimum is one hour, have a second job ready. A $125 faucet plus a second small fix inside the same hour costs the same as the faucet alone.
- **Send photos before the visit.** Denver Handy Co. says estimates scoped from a description and photos usually land within 10 to 15% of the actual time. A photo beats a surprise after the wall is open.
- **Ask about travel before you book.** Two metro operators charge no travel fee in Aurora. If a quote from farther out adds one, that's a real price difference.

How much does a handyman cost in Omaha?

The short answer. In Omaha, a handyman runs $40–$80 an hour, plus materials (HomeBlue, Omaha). A half day — four hours — runs $160–$310, and a full day runs $320–$610. Most small jobs hit a minimum first: Thumbtack puts minimum rates at $60–$100, so one tiny fix rarely bills at one hour.

This is where people overpay. A 20-minute fix booked on its own pays the minimum, plus the drive across town. The same fix on a half-day list — a faucet, a disposal, a patch, a sticking door — just takes its share of the $160–$310. Omaha’s floor sits lower, but the math is the same: one trip should carry as much work as you can hand over.

What changes the price in Omaha?

The list, or the lack of one. Write the small jobs down as they happen. In West Omaha (68144), one visit can cover a running toilet, a loose railing, and the gutters. Booked one at a time, each one pays its own minimum.

November is gutter and leaf season here. Big trees in West Omaha and Northwest Omaha (68142) drop a lot of leaves, and full gutters freeze that way. Gutter cleaning in Omaha runs $60–$210, priced at $0.50–$0.90 a linear foot (HomeBlue, Omaha). Leaf removal runs $40–$280, and raking alone runs $30–$50 an hour (HomeBlue, Omaha). Both are cheaper now than after the first snow sits on them.

Nebraska registers contractors, it does not license handymen. There is no state handyman license. But anyone doing more than $5,000 of construction or repair work in a year has to register with the Nebraska Department of Labor, and electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work carry their own licenses. So a handyman can patch, hang, assemble, and repair — a panel rewire or a gas line is a licensed trade’s job. Some Omaha work also needs a permit; HomeBlue notes an exterior door replacement sometimes does, and decks of a certain size and height can too.

Who you hire. Nationally, a self-employed handyman runs $50–$80 an hour and a company handyman runs $75–$125 (HomeGuide, national). Omaha’s $40–$80 range covers both ends of that. A company crew costs more and usually guarantees the work. A solo operator costs less and books out faster in fall.

How to pay less without a worse job

  • Bundle the list. One four-hour visit at $160–$310 beats four separate calls, each hitting a $60–$100 minimum.
  • Get the fall work done before snow. Gutters at $60–$210 and leaves at $40–$280 are November prices. Ice and a ladder in January are a different quote.
  • Buy the parts yourself when you can. A handyman who buys materials may add a 20% to 50% markup, plus a trip charge (HomeGuide, national). The faucet or disposal sitting by the door removes both.
  • Ask the price shape before he starts. Hourly, flat per job, or hourly plus a minimum — all three are normal in Omaha. Not knowing which one you agreed to is where most bad bills come from.

How much does a handyman cost in Salt Lake City?

The short answer. In Salt Lake City, a handyman runs $40 to $80 an hour on HomeBlue, with 4 hours at $170 to $320 and 8 hours at $340 to $640, plus materials. Salt Lake operators posting their own rates charge $65 an hour with a $100 minimum, and $70 an hour plus a $25 trip charge. Get the shape of the price in writing — hourly, flat, or minimum — before anyone starts.

This is where people overpay. A 30-minute fix booked alone still pays the minimum — $100 at one Salt Lake operator above, before materials. The same fix as the third item on a half-day list costs its share of $170 to $320 and nothing extra for the drive. The list is the discount in this trade.

What changes the price in Salt Lake City?

The guides disagree, and the gap is real money. HomeBlue puts Salt Lake at $40 to $80 an hour. A Salt Lake operator writing in January 2026 puts professional work at $60 to $100+ and notes published estimates for this city span $35 to $147 an hour, with one guide averaging $98. Ask each one what the job costs in total, not just the hourly number.

Winter writes part of the list here. Airtasker’s Salt Lake page puts it straight: snowy winters and the spring thaw show up as problems around gutters, exterior trim, windows, and doors, and the freeze-thaw cycle makes gutter and ice-dam repairs a recurring job, not a one-off. In West Valley City (84120), that’s November work — seal the window and fix the trim before the cold settles in.

Old plaster is slower work than drywall. The Avenues, Yalecrest, and Sugar House are full of early-1900s to 1940s homes with original trim and plaster walls. Patching that kind of wall without damaging it is a different job from patching a newer build, and Airtasker puts several plaster repairs in an older home toward the top of its range.

Materials sit outside the hourly rate. HomeBlue’s Salt Lake table is labor only. Ask before the visit: is that hinge, patch compound, or trim piece in the price, or on top of it?

What can a handyman legally do in Utah?

Utah Code 58-55-305, effective May 7, 2025, lets a person do repair or improvement work without a contractor license when the whole job — labor, materials, and every change added later — comes to less than $7,000. Older guides still quoting a $3,000 cap are out of date.

Two limits still bite. Past $3,000, the person has to file a one-time affirmation with the state showing liability insurance, and workers compensation if they have employees. And work on a plumbing or electrical system, an HVAC system, or a gas appliance still has to be done by a licensed or certified person for that trade. A faucet swap is one thing. A new circuit is another.

How to pay less and still get it done right

  • Hand over the whole list at once. One Salt Lake operator says it in his own pricing notes: come up with a list, and it works out better for both of you. One $100 minimum spread over five small jobs beats five minimums.
  • Pick hourly or flat on purpose. We Got A Guy charges $70 an hour plus a $25 trip charge, or a set price — the set price locks the most you’ll pay for a job that stays inside the agreed scope. For a job with a clear end, flat is usually safer.
  • Send photos before the visit. A wide shot and a close-up tell the handyman what to bring. A return trip for one part is billable time you didn’t need to buy.
  • Do the winter jobs in one fall visit. Gutters, trim, windows, and doors are one list in Salt Lake, not four calls spread across the cold months.

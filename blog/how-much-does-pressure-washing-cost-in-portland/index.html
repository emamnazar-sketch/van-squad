How much does pressure washing cost in Portland?

The short answer. In Portland, pressure washing a house runs about $600 on average on HomeBlue's Portland guide — $400–$800 for a 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft house. Angi's Portland page puts it lower, at $433 on average ($265–$601). A driveway averages about $240 on HomeBlue, $170–$300 for a standard 40 x 20 ft driveway. Portland runs about 40% above the national average (Angi). Oregon has no sales tax, so the quote is the price you pay.

This is where people overpay. A $433 quote and an $800 quote for "a house wash" can both be real — one is Angi's surveyed average, the other the top of HomeBlue's range for a bigger house. Ask what each one measured: house size, stories, or square feet of wall. Thumbtack's Portland per-square-foot figure is per square foot of your property size, not of the surface washed.

What changes the price in Portland?

Moss and algae, mostly. An Oregon house-washing guide puts it plainly: mild temperatures, heavy rain, and tree shade make this a moss and algae factory, and Portland-metro pricing runs about 40% above the national average partly because of that growth. A green north wall in Bethany (97229) in fir shade all winter is solution and dwell time, not a quick rinse.

Stories and size set the base. HomeBlue climbs by house size, up to $1,100–$1,900 at 4,000–5,000 sq ft. Angi prices two-story Portland homes at $470–$1,640 against $175–$1,170 for one story. Height and tight access push a quote up.

The surface decides the method — and the method sets the price. Brick runs $0.10–$0.50 a square foot in Portland on Angi, stucco $0.35–$0.60, wood $0.35–$0.65. Soft washing costs more per square foot than a plain rinse — $0.20–$0.80 against $0.15–$0.30 on the Oregon guide — because the solution does work plain water can't.

Should your house be pressure washed or soft washed?

Concrete takes high pressure. Siding doesn't. The Oregon guide sets them side by side: soft washing runs under 500 PSI with a solution that kills mold, mildew, and algae at the root, and lasts 1–2 years; pressure washing runs 1,300–3,000 PSI, is right for concrete, and on siding can force water behind panels, strip paint, and gouge wood.

Hot water is the upsell to price carefully. HomeBlue notes power washing — heated water — costs 10–15% more than cold-water Portland prices. It earns that on grease and oil. On moss film, it usually doesn't.

How to pay less and still get it clean

  • Bundle surfaces in one visit. House, driveway, and front walk in one stop — the crew is already set up. A 200 sq ft deck or patio runs $80–$120 as its own job (HomeBlue); as an add-on it should cost less.
  • Wash once a year, after the wet season. Angi puts the best window at March to November, with siding in spring or fall. One wash after winter clears the whole moss season.
  • Get the measuring stick in writing. By house size, by story, or per square foot — and per square foot of what? The two Portland guides differ mostly on this, not on the work.
  • Send photos of the worst spots. The green north wall, the mossy walk, the shaded steps in Tigard (97224) or Hillsboro (97123). A photo quote beats a surprise on the day.

Frequently asked questions

How much does pressure washing cost in Portland?

A house wash averages about $600 on HomeBlue's Portland guide ($400–$800 for a 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft house); Angi puts the Portland average at $433 ($265–$601). HomeBlue's Portland jobs run $80–$1,900 by task.

How much does it cost to pressure wash a driveway in Portland?

About $240 on average for a 40 x 20 ft driveway, $170–$300 (HomeBlue). Angi puts Portland driveways at $120–$300. Bigger drives climb fast: 100 x 20 ft runs $430–$760 on HomeBlue.

Should my siding be pressure washed or soft washed in Portland?

Soft washed. The Oregon house-washing guide says soft washing — low pressure under 500 PSI plus a solution — kills algae and mold at the root and lasts 1–2 years, while high pressure on siding can force water behind panels, strip paint, and gouge wood. Save high pressure for concrete.

How often should you pressure wash in Portland, and when?

Once a year (Angi), in the March to November window — siding and driveways in spring or fall. Homes shaded by firs, maples, or alders can need cleaning every 6–12 months because moss and algae grow back faster there (Oregon guide). A Portland-area operator backs that up: most Portland properties benefit from a yearly wash, thanks to the wet climate and heavy moss growth.

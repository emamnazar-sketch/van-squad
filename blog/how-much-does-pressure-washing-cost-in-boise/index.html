This is where people overpay. A $199 quote and a $500 quote can both be honest — one is a starting price for a smaller job, the other is a full 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft house. Ask what size and which surfaces the quote covers before you compare the totals.

What changes the price in Boise?

Dust, pollen, and wildfire ash. Fine dust and pollen from sagebrush and farmland sit on siding and concrete most of the year, and late-summer smoke leaves ash on shaded north walls (That 1 Painter, Treasure Valley). A light film is a quick wash. A wall that went through fire season untouched takes solution and dwell time.

Old wood in the North End, newer builds in Meridian. Older North End (83703) homes have chalkier paint and original wood siding that will not take maximum pressure. Newer builds in Meridian, Eagle, and Star can take a stronger wash (That 1 Painter). Same size, different method, different price.

Size, height, and access. HomeBlue prices a Boise house by size — $400–$700 at 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft, up to $1,000–$1,700 at 4,000 to 5,000 sq ft. Two stories, tight access, and landscaping in the way push a quote up.

Hot water costs more. Power washing uses heated water, and HomeBlue puts that at 10–15% more in Boise. You want it for grease and oil on a driveway in West Boise (83704). You do not need to pay for it on plain dust.

Should your house be pressure washed or soft washed?

Concrete driveways and walkways get high pressure. Painted siding and stucco get a soft wash — lower pressure with cleaning solution (That 1 Painter, Treasure Valley).

That split matters in Boise. Blast chalky North End paint and you strip it. Soft wash a Meridian driveway and the dust film comes off, but oil spots usually stay. A good quote names the method for each surface, in writing.

How to pay less and still get it clean

  • Bundle the flat work in one visit. Driveway, walkway, and patio in one stop in Meridian (83646) beats separate trips, each with its own starting price.
  • Wash after fire season, not before it. Thumbtack's Boise page says a warm, sunny day is best, after the rainy season has passed. Waiting until the smoke clears means the wash is not coated in ash again a week later.
  • Skip the hot-water upcharge unless you need it. Plain dust and ash do not need heated water. Oil and grease do — that is the 10–15% worth paying.
  • Ask about a multi-service discount. Window Genie of Boise advertises 10% off multi-service work on its Boise price page. Other companies will not offer it unless you ask.

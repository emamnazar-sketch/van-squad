How much does a handyman cost in Bismarck?

The short answer. In Bismarck, a handyman runs $50 to $90 an hour on HomeBlue’s Bismarck page, plus materials. A half day — 4 hours — runs $180 to $340, and a full 8-hour day runs $360 to $680. One small job on its own usually pays the first hour in full, so a list of three or four small jobs in one visit is where your money goes furthest.

This is where people overpay in Bismarck. They book one 20-minute fix, pay the full first hour, and then book the next small fix the same way next month. Put the fixes on one list. A thermostat swap, a running toilet, and a door that sticks are one visit, not three.

Ask how the quote is built before anyone starts. Hourly suits a mixed list with no clear end. Flat rate suits a job with a clear end — a faucet, a toilet, a ceiling fan. If a quote mixes the two, get which jobs are flat and which are on the clock, in writing.

What changes the price in Bismarck and Mandan?

Four things move a Bismarck quote more than anything else:

  • How the visit is used. HomeBlue’s Bismarck hours table drops the per-hour cost as the visit gets longer — 1 hour at $50 to $90, 4 hours at $180 to $340. One long visit beats three short ones.
  • Flat vs. hourly. Thumbtack’s North Dakota page puts a toilet replacement at $75 flat and a faucet replacement at $125 flat. Those are clear-end jobs. Open-ended work — find the leak, figure out why the door moved — goes on the clock.
  • Materials. HomeBlue’s Bismarck hours are labor only, plus materials on top. A faucet you supply and a faucet the handyman supplies price differently, and the markup question is fair to ask.
  • Licensed-trade work. North Dakota has no state handyman license, but a single job over $4,000 needs a state contractor license, and plumbing and electrical work have their own license tracks at any price, per Housecall Pro’s North Dakota guide. A handyman who tells you a big job needs a licensed contractor is telling you the law, not upselling you.

One rate warning. Koira puts a typical North Dakota job at $358, built from national guides with only the labor share scaled by the state median wage — an editorial estimate, not a survey of Bismarck quotes. It’s shown here only so you aren’t confused if you see it in search results.

Before you book, check three plain things:

  • Your list, in order. Put the job that bothers you most first. If time runs out, the rest can wait for the next visit.
  • Who supplies parts. Say which faucets, thermostats, or hardware you already have, so nobody makes a store run on your clock.
  • What happens in a snow week. Ask how rescheduling works when a storm hits on your visit day.

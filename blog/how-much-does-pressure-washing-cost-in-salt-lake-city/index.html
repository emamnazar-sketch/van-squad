How much does pressure washing cost in Salt Lake City?

The short answer. In Salt Lake City, pressure washing a house averages about $500 on HomeBlue — $400 to $700 for a 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft house. A standard 40 x 20 ft driveway averages about $210, at $150 to $270. A Sandy operator tells customers to set aside $200 to $500 for a typical property. Spring is the busy season here: winter road salt and inversion film need to come off before they sit through another year.

What changes the price in Salt Lake City?

Winter writes the spring list. One Salt Lake operator calls this one of the busiest Wasatch Front jobs each spring, right after the snow lifts: winter salt, snowmelt runoff, and pollen coat driveways and sidewalks. A Utah guide says the same for northern Utah: road salt does a number on driveways and lower siding, and it corrodes over time. The city averages 51.9 inches of snow a year, so a Sandy (84094) driveway carries a full winter of it by March.

Inversion film and hard water add their own layer. The same guide blames part of that spring wash on inversion-season pollution film. If your sprinklers hit the house, Utah's hard water leaves a chalky white buildup that etches in if it sits. That's pre-treatment time on a quote, not a quick rinse.

Size and stories set the base price. HomeBlue climbs by house size: $400 to $700 at 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft, up to $900 to $1,700 at 4,000 to 5,000 sq ft. Stories, tight access, and limited water access push it up. Heated water — power washing, strictly speaking — adds 10 to 15% over that guide's cold-water prices. It earns it on oil and grease, not plain dust.

Why older Salt Lake homes get a soft wash, not a blast

The method matters here, and the housing stock is why. Salt Lake City's median home was built in 1965. A local painter who washes across the valley puts it plainly: historic homes in The Avenues and Sugar House have chalky paint and softer joints that won't tolerate maximum pressure, while newer builds in Draper and Herriman can take a harder wash. His crews soft wash painted siding and stucco and save high pressure for concrete. A Utah guide agrees: concrete takes 2,500 to 3,500 PSI; stucco is soft wash only. If a quote doesn't name the method for your surface, ask.

How to pay less and still get it clean

  • Book the spring wash early. Masters' Guild puts peak demand in June, July, and August; spring books easier. The salt argument points the same way — get the winter film off in spring, not after another freeze.
  • Bundle surfaces in one visit. Driveway, front walk, patio — one stop. A 200 sq ft deck or patio runs $70 to $100 as its own job on HomeBlue; as an add-on, it should cost less than booking it alone.
  • Send photos of the worst spots. The white sprinkler haze, the salt-darkened driveway edge in Sandy (84094), the shaded north wall. A photo quote beats a surprise on the day.
  • Ask what the square-foot price measures. Surface washed, or your whole property? Thumbtack's note for the area makes the point: they aren't the same number.

How much does it cost to pressure wash a driveway in Salt Lake City?

About $210 on average for a 40 x 20 ft driveway, $150 to $270 (HomeBlue). A 2026 Salt Lake City table puts driveways at $100 to $350, and a Utah guide puts a standard 2-car driveway at $100 to $180, salt buildup included.

How often should you pressure wash in Salt Lake City, and when?

Once a year for most valley homes. Window Genie of Sandy advises a house washing every 12 to 18 months, and a Utah guide puts driveways and sidewalks at 1 to 2 times a year. Spring does the important work: road salt, snowmelt, and inversion film come off before another season.

Should my house be pressure washed or soft washed in Salt Lake City?

Soft washed, if it's siding, stucco, or painted wood — local operators keep high pressure for concrete. A Utah guide rates concrete at 2,500 to 3,500 PSI and stucco at soft wash only. Older paint in The Avenues and Sugar House is a soft-wash job, not a blast.

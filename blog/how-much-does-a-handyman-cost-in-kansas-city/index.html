How much does a handyman cost in Kansas City, Kansas?

The short answer. In Kansas City, Kansas, a handyman runs $50 to $90 an hour on HomeBlue’s Kansas City, Kansas page, materials on top. A half day (4 hours) runs $180 to $350, and a full day (8 hours) $370 to $690. Small jobs often price flat instead: on Thumbtack’s Overland Park page, a toilet replacement, a ceiling fan or light fixture, and a garbage disposal each run $75 flat, a faucet replacement $125, and minimum charges sit at $60 to $100.

This is where people overpay. One small job on its own pays the minimum and the trip. The same job on a half-day list just takes its share of the $180 to $350 — and four jobs priced one by one can cost more than the half day that covers all four.

What changes the price in Kansas City, Kansas?

Check which side of the river your quote comes from. These are two different cities, and the price pages split the same way. HomeBlue puts both sides at $50 to $90 an hour, but the half day differs — $180 to $350 on the Kansas page, $190 to $360 on the Missouri page. Angi’s Missouri page puts a typical project at $337 on average. Those figures aren’t wrong. They just aren’t a Kansas City, Kansas quote.

How the visit is priced matters more than the job itself. HomeBlue prices by the hour, and the per-hour cost drops as the block gets longer — four hours at $180 to $350 isn’t four times the one-hour price. Thumbtack’s Overland Park page makes the same point from the other side: bundle your tasks into one visit and you get the most out of the minimum fee.

Older houses carry longer lists. Homes.com’s city guide notes housing in Strawberry Hill and Riverview dating back to the mid-to-late 1800s, a lot of mid-20th-century houses, and a stock of bungalows and American Foursquares. Argentine, Rosedale, and Turner hold the same kind of houses. Old trim, old doors, old fixtures. The work is rarely one big job — it’s a page of small ones, exactly what a half-day booking is built for.

November is winter-prep season in Wyandotte County. Homes.com’s Argentine guide puts the average winter low at 23°F and annual snowfall at 24 inches. The jobs that matter now are plain: an outdoor faucet cover before a hard freeze, drafts around doors, a storm door that still won’t latch. All of it costs more as an emergency call in January than as a line on a November list.

Small jobs and licensed jobs are different conversations in Kansas. Kansas has no statewide handyman license. Housecall Pro’s Kansas guide is specific about Wyandotte County: Kansas City, KS wants every handyman to hold a city occupational tax license, and structural work needs a residential general contractor license. Ask about the city license before you book.

How to pay less and still get the work done

  • Write the whole list before you call. Doors, patches, fixtures, the lot. One visit, one minimum, one trip instead of three.
  • Ask which jobs price flat. A toilet at $75 flat and a faucet at $125 flat can beat an open hourly clock.
  • Supply the parts when you can. HomeBlue’s prices are labor — materials sit on top. A fixture already in the house saves a shopping trip billed as time.
  • Stay flexible on timing. HomeBlue lists flexible scheduling and one-day jobs as things that pull a price down, and emergency calls and permit work as things that push it up.

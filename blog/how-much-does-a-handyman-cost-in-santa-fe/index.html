How much does a handyman cost in Santa Fe?

The short answer. A handyman in Santa Fe bills an estimated $45 to $65 an hour, per BIG WORKS’ modeled rate built from the Santa Fe median handyman wage of $21.08 an hour. That figure is a model, not a survey — Santa Fe has no published citywide hourly average. Most small jobs land on a minimum or a flat price first: Thumbtack’s Santa Fe page puts minimums at $60 to $100, with flat jobs like a faucet swap at $125 and a ceiling fan or light fixture at $75. Bundle your list into one visit. That is where the money is saved here.

Read the table the way a Santa Fe homeowner should. The hourly rows disagree because they measure different things. BIG WORKS takes the Santa Fe wage — $21.08 an hour for a general maintenance worker, from the Bureau of Labor Statistics — and multiplies it by 2.2 to 3.0 to cover the truck, insurance, and overhead. That is an estimate with its method showing, not a quote anyone gave. A real company with insurance will sit above a solo operator, and a flat price for a known job beats an hourly guess almost every time.

This is where people overpay in Santa Fe. They book one small job at a time. A $75 fixture swap on its own still carries the trip and the minimum. Put the fixture, the sticking door, and the faucet on one list, and you pay one trip for three fixes.

Why do adobe and older Santa Fe homes change the quote?

A lot of Santa Fe work is not hard. It is slow. Adobe walls, vigas, and older trim do not take a screw or a patch the way drywall and pine do, and a handyman who knows these houses prices the extra care in. A drywall-style patch price from a national page will not hold on an adobe wall — ask how the repair will be finished, not just what it costs.

The same goes for doors and windows in older homes around ZIP 87501. Frames shift over time. A door that sticks in July may need planing, not a new hinge, and that is a longer job than the ten-minute fix it looks like. Get the diagnosis in the quote, in plain words, before the work starts.

What about the cooler on the roof?

Many Santa Fe homes run an evaporative cooler, and seasonal start-up and shut-down work lands on a lot of handyman lists here. That is a real local job in a way it is not in most of the country. Ask up front whether your handyman does roof work and cooler pads, or whether that job goes to a heating and cooling company. Don’t assume it is on the list — roof access changes the price and the crew.

If the cooler needs a repair beyond pads and a belt, you are probably in specialist territory. Paying a handyman’s minimum to be told that is still cheaper than guessing, but ask on the phone first and save the visit for the jobs he can finish.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a handyman cost in Santa Fe?

An estimated $45 to $65 an hour, per BIG WORKS’ modeled Santa Fe rate built from the local $21.08 median wage. That is a model, not a surveyed average. Most small jobs start at a $60 to $100 minimum or a flat price, per Thumbtack’s Santa Fe page.

How much is a faucet replacement in Santa Fe?

Thumbtack’s Santa Fe page lists faucet replacement at a $125 flat rate. That is labor on its standard list — your faucet itself is extra unless the quote says otherwise. Ask before the visit, not during it.

Why is there no firm Santa Fe hourly average?

No source publishes a surveyed citywide average for Santa Fe. The city figure here is modeled from the Bureau of Labor Statistics wage for Santa Fe. Real quotes will split by operator — a solo handyman at the low end, an insured company higher. Get two quotes for anything over a few hundred dollars.

Should I pay by the hour or a flat price in Santa Fe?

Flat, when the job is known — a fixture, a disposal, a toilet swap. Hourly makes sense for a mixed list where nobody can know the time until the wall is open, and even then, ask for a cap. Never leave an hourly job open-ended.

Does a handyman work on adobe walls in Santa Fe?

Some do, and it is worth asking directly. Adobe patching and drilling take different handling than drywall. A handyman who does this work regularly in 87501 will say so plainly. If he hesitates, that is your answer — get someone who knows the material.

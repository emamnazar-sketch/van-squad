How much does a handyman cost in Rapid City?

The short answer. In Rapid City, plan on $50 to $70 an hour billed — that is BIG WORKS’ modeled estimate for the Rapid City metro, built from the local median wage of $22.65 an hour, not a survey of quotes. A service call or small job in South Dakota runs $100 to $250 on the same page. HomeAdvisor’s Rapid City trim page puts a handyperson at $60 to $65 an hour. Materials are extra, and a minimum charge usually decides what one tiny fix really costs.

This is where people overpay in Rapid City. They book one $100-to-$250 service call for one small job, then book a second call next week for the job they forgot. The trip is the expensive part, not the screw.

Write the whole list before you call. A sticking door, a loose fixture, and a drywall patch in one visit share one trip. Three visits pay for three trips. Ask which jobs price flat and which run by the hour, and get that in writing before the work starts — a flat furniture assembly price on Thumbtack’s Rapid City page ($120 to $150) and an hourly drywall quote aren’t measured the same way, so don’t compare them as if they were.

Why does a November list matter in the Black Hills?

A Rapid City roofing company puts the local weather plainly: the Black Hills get frequent hailstorms, strong winds, heavy snow, and extreme temperature changes. Your house feels all four, in the small places first.

Caulking opens up. Doors shift and stop latching. A gutter that was fine in September sags under the first heavy snow if a bracket was already loose. None of that is roof work, and none of it needs a big crew. It’s handyman work — if it gets done before winter turns a loose bracket into a pulled-down gutter.

So walk the outside of your home in ZIP 57701, 57702, or 57703 this week. Note the cracked seal, the sticking gate, the trim that moved. That walk is your list — one November visit costs less than one emergency call in January.

What can a handyman legally do in South Dakota?

South Dakota has no statewide handyman license and no statewide general contractor license, and the state does not test handymen for competency. That surprises people. It doesn’t mean anything goes.

Almost every contractor doing construction or repair work in South Dakota must hold a Contractor’s Excise Tax License from the Department of Revenue and pay the state’s 2% excise tax on gross receipts. Past that, cities set their own rules — Rapid City licenses general contractors locally, per Housecall Pro’s South Dakota guide. Electrical and plumbing work are different: both trades are licensed by the state, with their own exams and rules.

Ask two plain questions before you book. Do you hold the excise tax license? And is any part of my list electrical or plumbing work that needs a licensed trade? A good handyman answers both without blinking, and hands the trade work to the right person.

Before you book, check three plain things:

  • The minimum. Ask what the smallest bill looks like. Nationally, Housecall Pro puts a minimum at $125 to $200 — a longer list costs little extra.
  • Materials. Ask if parts are billed at cost, marked up, or supplied by you. The hourly figures above are labor thinking, not parts.
  • The list, in order. Put the job that bothers you daily first. If time runs short, the small cosmetic jobs wait, not the door that won’t latch.

Does a Rapid City handyman need a license?

There is no statewide handyman or general contractor license in South Dakota. A handyman doing repair work still needs a state Contractor’s Excise Tax License, Rapid City licenses general contractors locally, and electrical and plumbing work need state-licensed trades. Ask about all three before the visit.

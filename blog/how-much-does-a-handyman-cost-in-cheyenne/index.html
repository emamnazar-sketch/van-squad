How much does a handyman cost in Cheyenne?

The short answer. A handyman in Cheyenne runs $40 to $80 an hour on HomeBlue's Cheyenne page. BIG WORKS' modeled billed rate for Cheyenne is $50 to $70 an hour, built from the local wage rather than quoted from jobs. Two Cheyenne operators who publish their own rates sit right in that band: one at $80 an hour with a one-hour minimum, one at $70 an hour plus a $50 travel fee. The trap is the minimum. A $50 job on paper can still bill as a full first hour plus travel, so ask what the visit costs, not just what the hour costs.

Read the two Cheyenne operator rows closely. Fixation charges $80 an hour, parts at cost, with a one-hour minimum that covers dispatch and travel. This is where people overpay in Cheyenne. They hire for one small job at a time — a latch this month, a patch next month — and pay a minimum on every visit. A list fixes that. Write down every small repair in the house, book one visit, and work the list top to bottom.

Why does the wind run up your handyman bill in Cheyenne?

Cheyenne sits at about 6,100 feet on the high plains, and it is one of the windiest cities in the country. Fixation's Cheyenne page says it plainly: fences, gates, siding, garage doors and roofs take the punishment. Quality Home Inspections puts average wind around 15 mph with gusts regularly over 50 to 60 mph, and notes the damage is often gradual — a shingle lifting a little, a seal giving up, a siding panel loosening.

That changes what a smart Cheyenne list looks like. Put the outside jobs first in spring: the loose fence panel, the gate that no longer latches, the siding piece that rattles. Those are cheap fixes in April. Left through a winter of gusts, a loose panel becomes a broken panel. Interior jobs — the drywall patch, the drawer, the trim — can wait for the months when nobody wants to work outside anyway.

What does coverage in ZIP 82003 actually cover?

Our directory's Cheyenne handyman coverage sits in ZIP 82003, on the south side of the city. That is South Cheyenne and Fox Farm-College country. South Cheyenne grew up south of the Union Pacific tracks, and most of its homes were built from the late 1800s to the 1930s — smaller bungalows and ranches, many of them in the South Cheyenne historic district, listed on the National Register in 2006. Fox Farm-College, home to Laramie County Community College, mixes ranch and manufactured homes with commercial strips along South Greeley Highway.

Old, small homes are handyman homes. A 1920s bungalow does not need a remodel. It needs a door planed so it shuts, a window that latches, plaster patched, a faucet that stops dripping. Each job is small. The list is long. That is exactly the work the hourly-plus-minimum pricing above rewards you for bundling — one visit, six small fixes, one minimum paid once.

Do you need a licensed handyman in Wyoming?

Wyoming has no statewide handyman license. Housecall Pro's Wyoming guide says the state leaves nearly all contractor licensing to cities and counties, and Cheyenne is one of the cities that requires a local contractor license. The one statewide exception is electrical work: anyone doing electrical installation or repair needs a license from the Wyoming State Electrical Board, wherever they work.

So ask two plain questions before you book. Are you licensed in Cheyenne, if your work needs it? And is any part of this list electrical? Swapping a faucet, patching drywall, fixing a gate — that is handyman work. Moving an outlet or replacing a panel is not, no matter who offers to do it while they are there.

How much is a half day or a full day of handyman work in Cheyenne?

HomeBlue's Cheyenne page puts 4 hours at $170 to $320 and 8 hours at $340 to $640, plus materials in both cases. That math is why a list pays: a half day booked once costs less than four separate one-hour visits, each carrying its own minimum.

What is a handyman minimum charge in Cheyenne?

Thumbtack's Cheyenne page puts minimum rates at $60 to $100. Housecall Pro's national minimum is higher, $125 to $200, labeled national. Why do Cheyenne fences and gates need fixing so often?

Wind. Cheyenne is one of the windiest cities in the country, with gusts regularly over 50 mph, and fences, gates and siding take the hit first. A loose panel or a sagging gate fixed early is an hourly job. The same panel after a winter of gusts is a rebuild.

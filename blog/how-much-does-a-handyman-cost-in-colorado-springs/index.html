How much does a handyman cost in Colorado Springs?

The short answer. A handyman in Colorado Springs runs $40 to $80 an hour on HomeBlue's city page, plus materials. A half day — four hours — is $170 to $330 at that rate, and a full eight-hour day is $350 to $660. Most small jobs pay a minimum first. Thumbtack's Colorado Springs page puts minimums at $60 to $100, and the HomeGuide figures quoted for the Springs put a minimum charge at $75 to $200. Put your small fixes on one list and share one visit. That is the whole saving.

This is where people overpay in Colorado Springs. They book one 20-minute fix, pay the full minimum, and book the next fix the same way next month. A sticking door, a patch in Briargate, and a loose fixture in 80909 are one visit, not three.

Why does the Springs winter change the list?

Colorado Springs sits high, and the cold finds the gaps. A Colorado seasonal maintenance guide for homes here puts the same small jobs at the top of the winter list: replace worn weatherstripping around doors and windows, refresh cracked caulk at frames, and wrap exposed pipes in unheated spots like garages and crawl spaces before a deep freeze does it for you.

Those are handyman jobs, and November is the cheap time to do them. Caulk cracked by the dry air and strong sun at this altitude lets cold air in all winter, and a furnace filter that should have been changed in October makes the system work harder on every cold night in Rockrimmon or Briargate. One visit with a list, before a hard freeze turns a small sealing job into a burst-pipe call.

Hail is the other Springs pattern. Storms leave loose trim and small siding dings that sit until water gets behind them. Those fixes are list work too — if the roof itself is damaged, that is a roofer's job, not a handyman's.

What can a handyman legally do in Colorado?

Colorado has no statewide handyman license. Housecall Pro's Colorado guide is plain on that point: there is no state handyman or general contractor license, and local rules in places like Colorado Springs cover permitted work.

Two lines still matter. Electrical work beyond a simple fixture swap, and plumbing work beyond a simple replacement, need a state-licensed electrician or plumber in Colorado. New wiring, a new circuit, or moving a pipe is not handyman work, whatever the quote says.

For the rest — patches, mounts, swaps, sealing, small installs — ask for insurance in writing and get the price shape in writing too: hourly, flat per job, or hourly plus a minimum.

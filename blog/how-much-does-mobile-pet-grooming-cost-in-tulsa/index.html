What changes the price in Tulsa?

Size first, coat second. HomeGuide's Tulsa page starts a salon full service at $40 for a small dog and $75 for a large one, and the mobile operator serving Greater Tulsa spreads its baths across $65–$150 by weight and service. A 12-pound dog is a short visit. A 90-pound double coat isn't.

Matting and coat condition. A dog on a regular schedule grooms faster than a dog who hasn't seen a brush in months. That time is the price, whether it's written as a flat range or a base price plus a matting fee.

Flea and tick season runs long here. Nutri-Green's Tulsa guide puts flea and tick season in Tulsa from April through October, peaking in late spring and summer once temperatures stay above 70°F. HomeGuide's Tulsa page prices a flea & tick treatment at $15 as an add-on. A dog who spends spring in a Broken Arrow backyard near a fence line or under a deck — the shaded spots Nutri-Green names — needs that add-on priced in, not sprung on you at the van.

One dog at a time costs more than a salon line. A salon runs several dogs through a day, hours apart. A van takes your dog start to finish, at your curb. You pay for the drive, the van, and the solo time.

Mobile or salon — what does the extra money buy?

A salon full service in Tulsa starts at $40 for a small dog and $75 for a large dog on HomeGuide's Tulsa page. The same page puts a mobile full service at about $75 for an average-size dog, and Thumbtack's national guide says mobile prices run higher because the van does the full job at your location.

What you get for it: no car ride with a nervous dog, no cage wait, and a dog who walks back into your own house when the cut is done. For an older dog, a dog who hates the car, or a house in Broken Arrow (74012) where the salon run eats half a morning, that difference is the point. For a calm dog and a tight budget, the salon still wins on price.

How to pay less and still get a good groom

  • Stay on a regular schedule. Care.com's Tulsa page notes that regular grooming keeps a coat in shape and cuts shedding and matting. A dog seen every few weeks is a shorter visit to quote.
  • Brush at home between visits. Loose undercoat and small tangles are free to fix on your porch. Matting at the van isn't.
  • Price the add-ons up front. On HomeGuide's Tulsa list the small pieces are cheap alone — nails $10, teeth $10, ears $10 — but they add up if each one is a surprise.
  • Tip on the work. HomeGuide's Tulsa page puts a normal tip at 15 to 20 percent of the total, with more for a dog who was extremely dirty or heavily matted.

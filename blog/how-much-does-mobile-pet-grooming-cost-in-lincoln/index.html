This is where people overpay in Lincoln. They compare a $25 salon bath for a small short-haired dog with a mobile full groom for a large long-haired dog and call the van expensive. Those are not the same job. On the Lincoln salon list alone, a bath runs from $25 to $62 before any haircut, just on size and coat length. Add the cut, the nails, and the trip to your driveway, and the gap closes fast.

Ask what the quote covers. Bath only, or bath plus haircut? Nails included, or $15 to $20 extra like the salon list? A matted coat is extra time, so send a photo when you ask — the surprise fee at the door is the one people remember.

Why does a Lincoln groom cost more in November?

Winter is coming, and coats know it. Dogs in Lincoln are growing in thicker coats right now, and the fur between the paw pads grows with it. That pad fur is the winter problem a groomer fixes in about a minute: snow packs into it, forms ice balls between the toes, and holds road salt against the skin.

A vet winter-safety guide puts it plainly — keep the hair on your dog’s feet trimmed short in winter, and it helps stop ice balls forming between the toes. The same guide says to wipe or wash paws after walks, because salt and de-icer are harsh on pads and dogs lick the residue off indoors.

So a November mobile groom in ZIP 68504 — Havelock and University Place, up by Nebraska Wesleyan and UNL’s East Campus — is not just a haircut. Ask for the paw trim as part of it. You get a clean dog in your driveway, and feet ready for the first salted sidewalk.

One winter catch: the van needs a driveway spot that is not sheeted with ice. On the coldest days, clear a short path first.

What should you check before you book in ZIP 68504?

In the Van Squads directory, Lincoln mobile pet grooming listings cover ZIP 68504 in Lincoln. That is the covered ZIP to search first if you are in Havelock, University Place, or the East Campus side of northeast Lincoln.

Before you book, check three plain things:

  • Water and power. Some grooming vans carry their own, some need a hookup at your home. Ask before the van arrives.
  • Vaccination records. Groomers commonly ask for proof of rabies vaccination. Have the record on your phone, not buried in a drawer.
  • Coat condition. Mats and a heavy shed coat add time and money. Say so up front and send a photo — a quote that already includes the de-matting will not grow at the door.

How much does pressure washing cost in Rapid City?

The short answer. In Rapid City, Aptera puts pressure washing at $385 and a driveway wash at $358 for the city. South Dakota guides run lower for single surfaces: a single-story house wash at $240 to $420, a two-car driveway at $110 to $200, and an in-town minimum around $150 to $250. The gap is real, so get the surfaces named in writing before you compare two quotes.

Aptera’s Rapid City driveway figure, $358, sits well above the South Dakota guide’s $110 to $200 for a two-car driveway. Those aren’t the same kind of number. Aptera gives one city figure with no size stated. The state guide prices a stated two-car driveway. Ask what size each number covers, and whether oil stains cost extra.

The two deck rows disagree too, $100 to $220 in one guide and $200 to $450 in the other, with Aptera’s Pennington County figure near $300 between them. Wood condition drives that spread.

This is where people overpay in Rapid City. They book one small surface at a time and pay the trip again and again. The guides put bundle savings at 10 to 20 percent, and adding a driveway to a house wash can run $100 to $175 less than booking it on its own.

Why do Black Hills homes cost more to wash?

Rapid City pricing looks similar to the rest of South Dakota on paper. Then the lot gets involved. The choice guide says hillside homes and steeper lots around Rapid City push labor costs up, because ladders, longer hoses, and slower rinsing take time.

Travel shows up fast once you leave town. The state price guide says a crew heading to Spearfish, Custer, or Hot Springs commonly adds $50 to $150. The choice guide puts Black Hills and rural minimums at $250 to $350, against $150 to $250 in town. If your place sits outside ZIPs 57701, 57702, and 57703, ask about the travel fee up front.

Cabins are their own job. Pine shade and needles stain log siding, and the choice guide puts most cabin washes at $500 to $1,200, with stain prep costing more. Log siding gets a soft wash at low pressure. High pressure can fuzz the wood and push water into the checks in the logs.

What should you check before you book in Rapid City?

Hard water is the quiet problem here. The choice guide says it leaves white spots on dark windows and chalky streaks on siding across Rapid City, and a crew that doesn’t plan for it can leave a house looking worse. Ask how the crew keeps glass from spotting, and whether a window rinse is in the quote.

Check these plain things too:

  • Method by surface. Siding and roofs get a soft wash at low pressure. Concrete can take high pressure. A quote that plans high pressure on vinyl siding is a reason to stop.
  • Tax. South Dakota taxes many services, per the choice guide. Ask whether the quote includes tax before you compare it with another crew’s number.
  • Water. In-town jobs in Rapid City run off your spigot for a dollar or two of utility water, per the state price guide. A crew hauling a tank to a place with no spigot may add $50 to $100.
  • Season. South Dakota crews work April through October in a normal year, per the state price guide, and the choice guide notes west river nights often dip below freezing by mid-October. A wash in late November in Rapid City isn’t a normal booking. Book the spring slot early instead, and disconnect your hose right after any late job so it can’t freeze on the tap.

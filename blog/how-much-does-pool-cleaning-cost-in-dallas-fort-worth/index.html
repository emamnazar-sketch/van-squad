How much does pool cleaning cost in Dallas–Fort Worth?

The short answer. A pool cleaning visit in Dallas runs around $30, with a range of $27–$32 a visit (HomeBlue’s Dallas page). Four visits a month come to $109–$128 on that same page. A weekly plan from a Dallas operator costs more — from $149 a month for partial and $209 for full service from Blue Science, and $250 a month flat from Golden Hour Pools. Budget $1,600–$1,900 a year for annual maintenance (HomeBlue).

Why the gap between $109 and $250? They are not the same job. The HomeBlue figure is a cleaning visit. A plan adds chemicals, equipment checks and a report, and full service adds netting and vacuuming. Blue Science charges $60 more for full than partial in Dallas — that $60 is netting and vacuuming. Golden Hour’s $250 includes filter cleaning. Blue Science charges it on top, at $179 every 3–4 months.

This is where people overpay. Compare what is in the quote, not just the number.

What changes the price in Dallas–Fort Worth?

Heat and hard water. Dallas tap water is hard and leaves calcium scale at the tile line, and July heat burns through chlorine in days (Blue Science’s Dallas page). You top the pool off, you add more hard water, scale builds again. Keeping the water balanced is most of the weekly job.

Trees and pollen. Pecans, live oaks and cedar elms over older Dallas neighborhoods — Lakewood, Kessler Park, Casa Linda and Forest Hills, the ones Blue Science names on its Dallas route — drop leaves all year and pollen in spring. Leaves that sit stain plaster and feed algae. A pool under those trees needs more work than one in full sun in a newer Plano subdivision.

Winter does not pause it. Leaves keep falling in January, and a freeze week can crack an unprotected pump. Blue Science says its winter visit gets shorter, but the water test, baskets and equipment check still happen weekly.

Your pool’s setup. HomeBlue lists a large pool, difficult access, trees and more accessories as reasons a quote lands higher, and a small or salt water pool as reasons it lands lower.

How to pay less without a green pool

  • Stay weekly in summer. Skipping July visits is how a regular plan turns into a cleanup bill. In Dallas, Golden Hour charges $350 for a one-time green-pool clean — more than a month of weekly service.
  • Take the partial plan only if you will do the work. At $149 a month, you handle netting and vacuuming and the pro handles chemistry, brushing, baskets and equipment checks. If you will not net the pool, the $209 full plan is cheaper than a rescue.
  • Empty the baskets between visits. After a storm, a packed basket strains the pump. Two minutes.
  • Ask about filter cleaning before you sign. Included, like Golden Hour’s plan, or billed every few months, like Blue Science’s $179? That line changes the real cost.

Frequently asked questions

How much does pool cleaning cost in Dallas–Fort Worth?

Around $30 a visit in Dallas, ranging from $27 to $32 (HomeBlue). Four visits a month run $109–$128. Weekly plans from Dallas operators run from $149 a month for partial and $209 for full service (Blue Science) to $250 flat (Golden Hour Pools). Annual maintenance runs $1,600–$1,900.

Are chemicals included in the price?

Yes, for routine chemicals in the Dallas plans above — both Blue Science plans and Golden Hour’s include them. Filter cleaning differs: included in Golden Hour’s $250, billed at $179 every 3–4 months by Blue Science.

Does my pool need weekly service, even in winter?

Weekly is the standard in Dallas. Heat burns off chlorine fast in summer, leaves fall all year, and a January freeze is when you want eyes on the pump. The winter visit gets shorter.

My pool is already green. What will that cost?

In Dallas, Golden Hour charges $350 for a one-time clean — two visits, chemicals included — and says dark green water can take more visits, quoted before extra work starts. Nationally, Thumbtack puts a one-time cleaning at $148–$413, most paying around $201 — a national figure, not a Dallas quote.
